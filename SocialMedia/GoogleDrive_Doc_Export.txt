FEDSAFE RETIREMENT
Global Social Media
Advertising Rules
A universal approval and execution standard for every social campaign
Service first. Education first. Trust always.
1. Purpose and Scope
These rules protect FedSafe Retirement’s identity across every paid social media campaign, on every current and future platform. They are intentionally campaign-neutral. They apply whether the objective is awareness, education, audience development, resource downloads, agency outreach, workforce education, ORA support, retirement readiness, consultation requests, event registrations, retargeting, or another approved initiative.
Governing question: Does this feel like a trusted federal retirement education organization serving federal employees and agencies? If not, it does not run.
	 
FedSafeRetirement.com is the baseline expression of the company’s real scope, services, positioning, and public identity. These rules govern how that established brand is translated into social advertising; they do not narrow, contradict, or replace the website’s approved positioning.
These rules govern strategy, copy, creative, forms, targeting, tracking, approvals, optimization, reporting, and asset ownership. Platform-specific tactics may change; the core standards may not. Compliance protects the work, but it does not lead the message or define FedSafe’s voice.
2. Brand North Star
FedSafe Retirement is a SAM.gov-registered federal contractor specializing in federal retirement education, workforce readiness, ORA guidance, retirement transition support, FERS and CSRS education, and federal and postal employee retirement preparation.
FedSafe’s long-term direction is to become a nationally recognized educational authority and Federal Retirement Intelligence Network: a credible source federal employees and agencies rely on for clarity, preparation, and workforce retirement readiness.
Messaging priority
Every audience-facing message should be developed in this order:
1.       Human and helpful
2.       Educational and relevant
3.       Calm, clear, and trustworthy
4.       Compliant in the background
Compliance is an essential guardrail, not the campaign idea, headline strategy, or tone of voice. Credentials, disclaimers, and required language must not overwhelm the audience benefit or make FedSafe sound corporate, legalistic, bureaucratic, sales-driven, or like a traditional financial advisor.
FedSafe must consistently feel
·       Service-first and education-first
·       Trustworthy, steady, informed, and reassuring
·       Modern, professional, and approachable
·       Premium without elitism
·       Institutional without bureaucracy
·       Authoritative without intimidation
·       Organized, clear, and experienced
FedSafe must never feel
·       Like an insurance funnel, TSP rollover operation, or product-promotion vehicle
·       Like a generic financial-advisory or seminar-marketing company
·       Aggressive, transactional, hype-driven, flashy, or overly motivational
·       Like an influencer or presenter-centered personality brand
·       Like a high-pressure retirement sales organization
Absolute marketing prohibition: FedSafe’s revenue model and product-led or asset-capture messaging must never appear in marketing. FedSafe may accurately market the planning, analysis, education, and support services reflected on its website—including federal benefits reviews, TSP strategy, retirement-income planning, FEGLI, FEHB, Medicare, Social Security, ORA, implementation support, and ongoing guidance—provided the message is centered on clarity, preparation, and the employee’s needs rather than a transaction or product pitch.

	4. Messaging and Calls to Action
Universal messaging rules
·       Lead with the person’s need, question, transition, or desired clarity—not with FedSafe’s credentials, compliance status, or organizational language.
·       Lead with education, assistance, readiness, clarity, or access to a useful resource.
·       Write like a knowledgeable, patient human helping another person. Use plain language and natural phrasing.
·       Avoid jargon, stiff corporate language, legalistic phrasing, inflated language, fear tactics, and manufactured anxiety.
·       Employee-facing copy must never sound like a financial advisor prospecting for assets or steering someone toward a transaction.
·       Agency-facing copy may be more formal, but it must remain direct, useful, service-oriented, and easy to understand.
·       Use urgency only when it reflects a factual deadline, date, or genuine capacity limit.
·       Never promise or imply guaranteed retirement, financial, attendance, consultation, or campaign outcomes.
·       Testimonials and success stories require verification and written permission and may not imply that another person will receive the same outcome.
·       Every retirement fact and material claim must be verified against a current primary federal source before publication.
Approved employee-facing calls to action
·       Register for a seminar or webinar
·       Download an educational resource
·       Schedule a one-on-one consultation
·       Watch educational content
·       Join email or SMS updates
·       Call or text FedSafe
Approved agency-facing calls to action
·       Request an agency briefing or consultation
·       Download FedSafe’s capabilities statement
·       Explore workforce education programs
·       Call or email FedSafe
·       Request information or a proposal
5. Independence and Accuracy Guardrails
·       FedSafe must always be presented as an independent federal retirement education organization.
·       A clear independence disclosure is required on landing pages and in any ad where a reasonable viewer could misunderstand FedSafe’s relationship to OPM, USPS, or another federal agency.
·       USPS, OPM, and other agency names may appear in plain text only when necessary for accurate context.
·       Government seals, agency logos, and visual devices that imply official affiliation, sponsorship, or endorsement are prohibited.
·       Copy may not describe FedSafe content as official government or agency instruction.
·       FedSafe’s status as a SAM.gov-registered federal contractor is a legitimate differentiator and may be highlighted prominently in both employee- and agency-facing campaigns when relevant.
·       The federal-contractor credential must be stated accurately and may not imply that FedSafe is endorsed by, operated by, or officially affiliated with the federal government or an agency.
·       Claims must be current, supportable, and specific. Exaggerated, misleading, comparative-superiority, or guaranteed-outcome claims are prohibited.
6. Logo and Credential Rules
Only approved marks: The supplied FedSafe Retirement logo and the supplied SAM.gov Registered logo are the only logos permitted in FedSafe advertising.
	 
·       The FedSafe Retirement logo is the primary brand mark.
·       The SAM.gov Registered logo is a secondary credential mark and must remain visually subordinate to the FedSafe logo.
·       The two approved logos may be placed next to each other or used separately. Adjacent placement is expressly permitted when the composition remains clean and intentional.
·       Proportional resizing is permitted. A logo may also be sharpened, upscaled, or cleaned to remove image noise only when its design, wording, color, shape, spacing, proportions, and visible content remain unchanged.
·       Neither logo may be recreated, redrawn, distorted, stretched, compressed, recolored, cropped, traced, generatively reconstructed, substituted, or otherwise redesigned.
·       The SAM.gov mark remains a credential supporting the FedSafe identity and may not be presented in a way that implies government endorsement.
·       No additional partner, platform, government, agency, vendor, or decorative logo may appear unless Mike gives explicit written approval for a specific use.
7. Visual and Presenter Standards
People and photography
·       Show realistic federal workforce demographics, generally ages 57–65 when portraying employees near retirement.
·       Use credible diversity, with intentionally stronger Black representation than generic stock libraries often provide.
·       All people shown in social media advertising must be casually professional: polos, long-sleeve button-up shirts without ties, khakis, and comparable relaxed workplace clothing.
·       Suits, suit jackets, sport coats, ties, and traditional financial-advisor wardrobe styling are prohibited in social media campaign creative.
·       Favor approachable settings, workforce education, consultation, and calm professional environments.
·       Avoid luxury-finance aesthetics, staged advisor imagery, seminar-stage energy, motivational-speaker poses, exaggerated smiles, and sales-team clichés.
Design
·       Use clean layouts, restrained typography, clear hierarchy, uncluttered spacing, and educational framing.
·       Avoid busy compositions, excessive overlays, exaggerated gradients, financial-services clichés, insurance aesthetics, and funnel-style creative.
·       Keep the offer and next action clear without making the creative feel urgent, loud, or transactional.
Partners and presenters
FedSafe is company-first. Mike Zaino, Ben Bailey, Brian Westrich, and Daniel French may appear as rotating client-facing educators when useful, but no individual should become the sole face of the brand. Presenters and imagery should rotate, and the educational value—not a personality—must remain central. Greg Lamm is not client-facing and must not be named, featured, or depicted in public-facing social advertising or campaign materials.
AI-generated imagery
AI-generated supporting imagery is allowed only after human review for realism, representation, dignity, accuracy, and brand fit. AI may never invent, redesign, or generatively reconstruct FedSafe’s logos, the SAM.gov credential, government seals, official documents, testimonials, or people presented as real customers or officials. Non-generative sharpening or noise reduction of an approved logo is allowed only when the result is visually identical apart from improved image clarity.
8. Lead Forms, Consent, and Follow-Up
Form length must match the value and intent of the offer. Events and consultations may require full data. Low-friction educational content should use a shorter form unless a longer form is specifically justified and approved.
Full-data fields for events and consultations
·       Full name
·       Email address
·       Personal cell phone number—not an office or agency number
·       Complete home mailing address when direct-mail follow-up is part of the approved strategy
·       Federal agency or employer
·       Selected event or requested service, as applicable
·       General retirement timeframe
·       The registration experience must clearly explain what information is being collected and how FedSafe intends to follow up.
·       Consent language for email, SMS, direct mail, tracking, and platform lead forms must be standardized, current, appropriate to the campaign, and reviewed before use.
·       Forms may not use deceptive design, hidden consent, or unnecessary fields that create friction without a defined business purpose.
·       All approved leads must flow into GoHighLevel and enter the correct follow-up workflow.
9. Targeting, Retargeting, and Data Use
·       Permitted targeting may include location, age, employer or job signals, relevant interests, and compliant first-party audiences.
·       Targeting must comply with platform rules and applicable requirements and may not use deceptive, invasive, discriminatory, or improperly obtained data.
·       Retargeting is allowed and encouraged for people who engage at any meaningful level, including website visits, ad engagement, video viewing, link clicks, form starts, resource use, and prior campaign interaction.
·       Retargeting should move people through a useful sequence of education, proof, resources, and appropriate next steps rather than repeating the same advertisement or call to action indefinitely.
·       After someone completes an intended action, suppress the now-irrelevant conversion advertisement and move the person into an approved next-stage audience when another useful message exists.
·       Use reasonable frequency controls, creative rotation, exclusions, and opt-out handling so retargeting remains persistent and effective without becoming intrusive or obnoxious.
·       Audience expansion, new data sources, new lookalike strategies, or material targeting changes require Mike’s approval.
10. Platform Adaptation
The core positioning, factual claims, approved offer, logo treatment, and service-first identity must remain consistent. Format, tone, visual pacing, copy length, and presentation should be adapted to the native expectations of each platform.
·       Google: trusted, stable, authoritative, informational, and clear.
·       Facebook and Instagram: warm, approachable, helpful, community-oriented, and human-centered.
·       LinkedIn: institutional authority, workforce-readiness leadership, federal-contractor credibility, and executive-level relevance.
·       YouTube: an educational media network and Federal Retirement Intelligence Network—not a personality channel or ‘Mike Zaino TV.’
·       Future platforms: adapt execution, never the brand’s underlying identity or compliance standard.
11. Ownership, Approval, and Change Control
Ownership
FedSafe must retain ownership and full administrative access to all advertising accounts, business managers, leads, contact data, pixels, conversion events, audiences, campaign history, copy, creative, landing pages, forms, reporting, and attribution data.
Approval authority
·       Mike Zaino gives final approval before any campaign, advertisement, landing page, form, or material creative change is launched.
·       The relevant partner verifies event, location, audience, agency, subject-matter, or operational facts when needed.
·       A vendor may optimize bids, placements, and budget allocation without new approval only within the approved campaign scope and total spending cap.
·       Any change to copy, creative, imagery, logos, offer, call to action, form fields, consent language, landing-page content, audience strategy, or campaign objective requires approval before publication.
Revision discipline
When one element is approved for revision, only that element may be changed. Unrelated copy, layout, color, spacing, imagery, logo treatment, or campaign logic must remain untouched unless the approval explicitly includes it.
12. Testing, Measurement, and Reporting
Testing should answer a defined question and should not weaken the brand merely to improve a surface-level metric. When practical, change one meaningful variable at a time so results are interpretable.
Measurement hierarchy
·       Primary conversion for the campaign’s objective
·       Lead quality and completeness
·       Cost per qualified action
·       Attendance or content engagement, when applicable
·       One-on-one consultations or agency conversations generated, when applicable
·       Performance by audience, geography, message, creative, placement, and format
·       Click-through rate, reach, impressions, and engagement as diagnostic—not standalone success—metrics
Reporting cadence
·       Weekly reporting during active campaigns, including spend, delivery, conversions, lead quality, notable changes, problems, and recommended actions.
·       A monthly consolidated review covering cross-campaign performance, insights, decisions, next tests, budget recommendations, and ownership of action items.
·       Material tracking failures, policy risks, account restrictions, overspend risks, or significant lead-quality issues must be reported promptly rather than held for the next scheduled report.
13. Pre-Launch Approval Checklist
1.       The campaign has one clearly defined audience lane and objective.
2.       The message passes the service-first, education-first test.
3.       No revenue-model, asset-capture, product-pitch, or high-pressure sales framing appears; approved planning and educational service topics remain centered on clarity and employee needs.
4.       Every material retirement fact has been checked against a current primary federal source.
5.       Agency names are accurate, plain-text references; no prohibited seals or logos appear.
6.       Only the approved FedSafe and SAM.gov logos appear; any resizing is proportional and any cleanup changes sharpness only. Adjacent placement is permitted.
7.       The creative meets FedSafe’s photography, diversity, casual-wardrobe, presenter, AI, design, and platform standards, and Greg Lamm is not shown or named.
8.       The call to action is approved for the relevant audience lane.
9.       The landing page, form fields, consent language, GoHighLevel routing, tracking, and suppression logic have been tested.
10.   FedSafe owns and can access every account, asset, audience, and data source involved.
11.   The budget and permitted vendor optimizations are documented.
12.   The relevant partner has verified facts when needed, and Mike has given final approval.
Final standard: If an asset feels too sales-oriented, too financial-advisor focused, too motivational, too cluttered, too personality-driven, or too close to government affiliation, revise it toward clarity, education, professionalism, trust, and calm institutional credibility.
